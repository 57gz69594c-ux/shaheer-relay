WHAT HAPPENED ON ROBINHOOD TODAY — wsNET/USDG market, and how to never be the victim
=====================================================================================

PLAIN-ENGLISH SUMMARY
- One operator (one contract, ~20 wallets feeding it) liquidated ~32 borrowers on the RH
  wsNET/USDG market and walked away with roughly $13K of liquidation bonus in a few hours.
- Our engine did NOT fire on these, and that was CORRECT: at the moment each victim was hit,
  the loan was still healthy on paper (health factor ~1.00001 to ~1.03). A normal liquidate
  call from us would have reverted and cost gas. They were not "missed" — they were not
  liquidatable by real market movement.

WHY THIS MARKET WAS VULNERABLE (the root cause, no recipe)
- The wsNET collateral was priced by a THIN on-chain oracle: it reads the spot price out of a
  small liquidity pool. Small pool = the price is cheap to move and moves a lot per dollar in.
- When the number a lending market trusts for "what is your collateral worth" comes from a
  place that one trader can shove around, the market itself is the weak point — not the borrower.
- The attacker exploited the ORACLE, not a bug in the borrower's wallet. The borrowers did
  nothing wrong except post collateral into a market that trusted a shovable price.

WHAT THE ATTACK LOOKED LIKE FROM THE OUTSIDE (outcome, not instructions)
- Everything the attacker needed happened inside a SINGLE transaction, at the very top of a
  block, so no one — including us — could react between the steps.
- For that one instant the market believed the collateral was worth less than the debt, so the
  liquidation was "legal" by the contract's rules. Then the price snapped back the same block.
- To any outside observer (and to our health checks a block earlier and a block later) the loan
  looked perfectly healthy. That is the whole trick: the underwater moment never exists on a
  normal, settled price — it is manufactured and erased in the same breath.
- I am deliberately NOT writing the sizing, ordering, or call sequence that manufactures it.
  That is an attack playbook and we do not build or hand out attacks. This report is defense only.

HOW MUCH / IS IT ILLEGAL (for your knowledge)
- Take: ~$13K bonus across 32 hits, before their gas and trading costs (net kept: unmeasured).
- Legality: in the US this can be charged as market manipulation / fraud (faking a price to take
  others' funds). Closest precedent is the 2022 Mango Markets case ($110M) — a 2024 conviction
  that a judge vacated in 2025, so the law here is genuinely unsettled.
- At ~$13K with anonymous wallets, prosecution is very unlikely. Realistic outcome: the market/
  oracle gets patched. Not legal advice.

HOW YOU AND YOUR FRIENDS STAY SAFE (the part that matters)
1. Before borrowing, ask ONE question: where does this market get my collateral's price? If the
   answer is a single small pool / spot AMM oracle — treat that market as unsafe to borrow in.
   Safe markets use deep aggregated feeds (e.g. Chainlink-style) with wide liquidity behind them.
2. Thin-oracle markets are fine to LEND stable into and fine to trade, but do not post volatile
   collateral and borrow against it there — that is exactly the position that got hit today.
3. Keep a fat buffer. Every victim today sat at health factor ~1.00–1.03. A loan run at 1.5–2.0+
   is far more expensive to force under and usually not worth an attacker's capital.
4. Prefer large, deep markets. The attack only pays when the pool is small enough to move cheaply.
5. If you must be in a thin market, watch it and be ready to add collateral or exit — these hits
   cluster fast once someone finds the market (32 in a few hours today).

OUR SIDE (already handled)
- Engine behaved correctly (did not fire on manufactured-underwater healthy loans).
- Separate real bug found & assigned: signed plans were being silently dropped by a policy/RPC
  error on 8 of these borrowers (run220). Fix in progress; it does NOT and will not manipulate any
  price — it only makes us fire cleanly when a loan genuinely crosses on real market/interest.
